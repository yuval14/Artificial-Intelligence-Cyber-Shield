## 1. Executive Program Overview

The Chief AI Officer (CAIO) Executive Certification Program is a 200-academic-hour, vendor-agnostic curriculum designed for experienced executives and senior professionals who are expected to lead enterprise AI strategy, governance, adoption, security, assurance, operating models, investment, and measurable value realization.

The program treats AI as an enterprise transformation capability rather than a standalone technology. It combines executive leadership, data strategy, AI architecture, governance, cybersecurity, risk management, responsible AI, agentic AI, testing and evaluation, procurement, economics, workforce transformation, board oversight, and a mandatory applied capstone.

- **Target audience:** Executives and senior professionals with 10+ years of professional experience.
- **Academic duration:** 200 academic hours. One academic hour is defined as 45 minutes.
- **Equivalent contact/structured learning time:** 150 clock hours.
- **Independent capstone effort:** 30-50 additional hours recommended and not included in the 200 academic hours.
- **Delivery modes:** Classroom, hybrid, or synchronous online.
- **Program level:** Executive / Advanced.
- **Technical prerequisite:** Programming is not required; participants should be comfortable with enterprise technology and digital transformation concepts.

### Program Design Principles

- Vendor agnostic: enduring concepts and decision frameworks take precedence over specific products.
- Executive oriented: focus on strategy, accountability, governance, risk, architecture, investment, and organizational outcomes.
- Applied: each domain includes cases, workshops, decision exercises, or implementation artifacts.
- Risk informed: security, privacy, safety, legal, ethical, operational, and third-party risks are integrated throughout.
- Evidence driven: AI adoption must be justified against non-AI alternatives and measured against explicit acceptance criteria.
- Lifecycle based: the CAIO governs AI from ideation and procurement through deployment, monitoring, change, and retirement.

## 2. Admission Requirements

- Minimum 10 years of relevant professional experience.
- Meaningful experience in management, technology, data, architecture, cybersecurity, risk, governance, operations, finance, consulting, transformation, or a related discipline.
- Experience participating in organizational decision-making, governance, program delivery, or enterprise change.
- Basic familiarity with information systems, business processes, data, and digital transformation.
- No mandatory programming or vendor certification prerequisite.

## 3. Program Learning Outcomes

1. Define the mandate, authority, accountability, organizational placement, and operating model of a CAIO.
2. Develop an enterprise AI strategy aligned with business or mission objectives.
3. Establish an integrated Data and AI operating model.
4. Assess organizational AI maturity, readiness, and transformation capacity.
5. Identify, prioritize, fund, and govern AI use cases and portfolios.
6. Transform existing business processes using AI or establish new AI-enabled processes.
7. Govern traditional ML, Generative AI, foundation models, multimodal systems, and agentic AI.
8. Establish an AI Management System and enterprise AI governance framework.
9. Integrate AI risk into enterprise risk management and decision-making.
10. Establish AI cybersecurity, resilience, identity, and secure lifecycle requirements.
11. Define data governance, model governance, knowledge governance, and AI asset accountability.
12. Design vendor-neutral enterprise AI reference architectures and integration principles.
13. Establish TEVV, evaluation, red teaming, assurance, acceptance, and continuous monitoring processes.
14. Manage AI procurement, third-party risk, concentration risk, portability, and exit strategies.
15. Build AI financial governance, FinOps, ROI, and value-realization mechanisms.
16. Lead AI literacy, workforce transformation, organizational change, and responsible experimentation.
17. Establish AI incident and crisis management capabilities.
18. Provide decision-useful AI reporting to executives and boards.
19. Defend, modify, postpone, reject, or terminate AI initiatives based on evidence.

## 5. Mandatory Capstone Project - AI Business Process Transformation

Successful certification requires each participant to design, document, present, and defend an applied AI Business Process Transformation Project based on the participant's organization or a realistic organizational context.

### Capstone Tracks

**Track A - Transform an Existing Business Process:** Select an existing organizational process and redesign it using AI. The candidate must document the AS-IS process and the AI-enabled TO-BE process.

**Track B - Introduce a New AI-Enabled Process:** Design and plan implementation of a new organizational process or capability enabled or materially improved by AI.

### Mandatory Capstone Components

1. Business or mission problem and strategic alignment
2. Stakeholder and governance context
3. AS-IS process model, where applicable
4. AI suitability assessment and explicit non-AI alternative
5. TO-BE AI-enabled process model
6. Human-AI task and decision allocation
7. Data requirements, data quality, ownership, provenance, lineage, and lifecycle
8. Vendor-neutral conceptual architecture
9. AI governance and accountability model
10. Risk assessment and risk-treatment plan
11. Cybersecurity and resilience requirements
12. Responsible AI and human-oversight assessment
13. Legal, privacy, IP, and records considerations
14. TEVV strategy and measurable acceptance criteria
15. Implementation roadmap: discovery → design → PoC → pilot → evaluation → approval → production → monitoring → improvement
16. Change-management, workforce, and AI-literacy plan
17. Financial/value analysis, TCO, ROI or mission-value metrics
18. KPIs and KRIs
19. Operational monitoring and incident model
20. Business continuity, rollback, human fallback, portability, and exit strategy

### Required Deliverables

- Executive Project Summary
- AS-IS process model (when applicable)
- TO-BE AI-enabled process model
- AI suitability and non-AI alternative assessment
- Business case
- Vendor-neutral conceptual architecture
- Data and governance requirements
- Risk and security assessment
- Responsible AI and legal/privacy assessment
- TEVV and acceptance plan
- Implementation and change roadmap
- Financial/value assessment
- KPI/KRI dashboard
- Executive presentation deck

### Final Presentation and Executive Defense

- **Executive presentation:** 20 minutes.
- **Panel defense:** 15 minutes.

The assessment panel should challenge the candidate on business value, AI necessity, architecture, data readiness, governance, security, risk, regulation, human oversight, cost, implementation feasibility, measurement, failure scenarios, and exit strategy.

Candidates must demonstrate the judgment to: **Approve → Modify → Delay → Reject → Terminate.**

## 11. Source and Benchmarking Note

The structure of this syllabus reflects benchmarking against executive technology, CIO/CDO/CDAIO, data-governance, AI-governance, and AI-management curricula, including themes commonly emphasized in executive information-systems and data leadership programs. The syllabus is independently structured around the distinct accountability of the Chief AI Officer.

In particular, the curriculum intentionally incorporates Data Strategy, Data Architecture, Data Governance, Data Quality, Master Data Management, Metadata, Lifecycle Management, Data Products, Literacy, Culture, and Operating Model topics while keeping the program centered on CAIO responsibilities rather than converting it into a CDO curriculum.

## 12. Defining Standard for a CAIO Graduate

A Chief AI Officer should not be certified merely for understanding AI technology. A successful graduate must demonstrate the ability to determine where AI creates organizational value, where it should not be used, how business processes should change, how AI should be governed and secured, how evidence should be generated before deployment, how performance and risk should be monitored, how humans remain accountable, and when an AI initiative should be changed or stopped.
