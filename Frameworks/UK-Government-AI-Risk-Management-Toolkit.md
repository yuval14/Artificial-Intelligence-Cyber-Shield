# UK Government AI Risk Management Toolkit

**Organization:** UK Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT)  
**Published:** September 8, 2026  
**Category:** AI Governance, Risk Management, Assurance, and Security  
**Official guidance:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-risk-management-toolkit/ai-risk-management-toolkit-guidance  
**Toolkit landing page and workbook:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-risk-management-toolkit

## Overview

The UK Government AI Risk Management Toolkit provides practical guidance for teams involved in the design, operation, procurement, and delivery of AI-enabled products and services. It is intended as a starting point for multidisciplinary teams implementing structured AI risk management across the full AI lifecycle.

The toolkit aligns with the UK Government Orange Book risk-management approach and is designed to support risk identification and assessment, risk treatment, risk monitoring, and risk reporting. It is also designed to work alongside established cyber-risk approaches, including the Cyber Assessment Framework (CAF).

## Core Components

The toolkit includes:

1. An AI risk-assessment guide.
2. Critical questions for identifying AI risks.
3. A workbook for recording risks, assessments, owners, and treatment actions.
4. An AI Risk Monitoring Dashboard for tracking the overall risk profile of an AI solution.

## Risk Areas

The guidance addresses risk categories including:

- Financial risk.
- Legal and regulatory compliance.
- Transparency and explainability.
- Fairness.
- Accountability and governance.
- Contestability and redress.
- Technical robustness.
- Security.
- Risks to people and the environment.

## Risk Assessment Approach

The toolkit uses likelihood and impact scoring on a 1-5 scale and calculates a risk score from likelihood and impact. It recommends combining quantitative and qualitative evidence, including historical data, model analysis, expert judgment, experimentation, and operational monitoring.

Risk appetite should be aligned with wider organizational risk appetite and, where appropriate, approved at board level. The guidance emphasizes continuous reassessment because AI systems, data, models, usage contexts, technical capabilities, and regulatory conditions evolve throughout the lifecycle.

## Cybersecurity Relevance

The toolkit explicitly includes AI security risks such as data poisoning, data leakage, and cyber attacks. It calls for secure-by-design practices, vulnerability testing before deployment, lifecycle security processes, incident response, disaster recovery, and business continuity. It also allows relevant Cyber Assessment Framework evidence to support AI risk assessment where that evidence has been expanded to cover AI.

## Recommended Use within the AI Cyber Shield

Use this toolkit as a practical public-sector AI risk-management layer alongside NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 23894, ISO/IEC 42001, the UK Cyber Assessment Framework, and AI-specific security frameworks. It is particularly useful for translating governance principles into a working risk register, explicit risk appetite, treatment decisions, ownership, continuous monitoring, and management reporting.
