# Australia Voluntary AI Safety Standard

## Overview

The **Voluntary AI Safety Standard (VAISS)** is Australian Government guidance for organizations that develop, supply, or use AI systems. It sets out 10 voluntary guardrails covering organizational accountability, risk management, cybersecurity and data governance, testing, human oversight, transparency, contestability, supply-chain information, records, and stakeholder impacts.

**Current-status note:** The Department of Industry, Science and Resources states that its *Guidance for AI Adoption*, published on October 21, 2025, updates and simplifies the VAISS. The VAISS page remains available as a reference to the original 10-guardrail model. For current Australian Government implementation guidance, consult the successor guidance as well.

## The 10 guardrails

1. **Accountability and governance:** Establish, implement, and publish an accountability process, including governance, internal capability, and a regulatory-compliance strategy.
2. **Risk management:** Identify and mitigate risks based on the AI system's use and impact, and review whether mitigations remain effective.
3. **System protection and data governance:** Protect AI systems and manage data quality, provenance, privacy, and cybersecurity.
4. **Testing and monitoring:** Test models and systems against defined acceptance criteria before deployment and monitor them for changes and unintended effects.
5. **Human oversight:** Enable human control or intervention across the AI system lifecycle.
6. **User information:** Inform end users about AI-enabled decisions, AI interactions, and AI-generated content.
7. **Challenge and contestability:** Provide ways for affected people and organizations to challenge AI use and contest decisions or outcomes.
8. **Supply-chain transparency:** Share information about data, models, and systems with other organizations in the AI supply chain so they can understand and manage risks.
9. **Records and evidence:** Maintain records, including an AI inventory and system documentation, that allow assessment of adoption and compliance with the guardrails.
10. **Stakeholder impacts:** Engage stakeholders and consider safety, diversity, inclusion, fairness, and the needs and circumstances of affected groups.

The guardrails are intended as ongoing organizational and system-level practices. The standard highlights supplier engagement and procurement because organizations often use AI systems developed by third parties.

## Cybersecurity and assurance relevance

The VAISS is useful as a governance and lifecycle reference for AI cybersecurity programs. Guardrail 3 directly addresses system protection, data governance, provenance, privacy, and cyber vulnerabilities. Guardrails 2, 4, 5, 8, and 9 support risk-based security decisions through recurring assessment, testing, human intervention, supply-chain transparency, and auditable records.

Organizations can use the guardrails to:

- Incorporate AI systems into inventories, risk registers, and governance processes.
- Set security and data-provenance expectations for AI suppliers.
- Define pre-deployment testing criteria and post-deployment monitoring.
- Record human-oversight mechanisms, escalation paths, and intervention procedures.
- Maintain evidence for internal assurance and applicable legal or regulatory requirements.
- Map responsible-AI governance requirements to technical controls and security testing.

The standard is voluntary guidance, not a cybersecurity control catalog, certification scheme, or substitute for applicable law. A mapping to the VAISS should be paired with technical threat modeling, secure development and deployment practices, incident response, and relevant security standards. The department notes alignment with international standards, including ISO/IEC 42001:2023 and NIST AI RMF 1.0.

## Relationship to the successor guidance

The department's current *Guidance for AI Adoption* presents six essential practices for safe and responsible AI governance and is described as an updated, simplified evolution of the VAISS. The two resources should therefore not be presented as independent, competing standards. Retain the VAISS when a comparison requires the original 10 guardrails; use the successor guidance for current Australian Government implementation material.
